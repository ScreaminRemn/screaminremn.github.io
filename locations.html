The World Tale of Creation Factions Locations Order of Names

Locations

Each entry has a bestiary, geographical locations of note, general descriptions of the landscape, and sub factions or groups of note.

Many entries at this time have yet to be discovered, but soon they shall be seen.

Fahroway

Fahroway is a land to the vast south-east of Mothua, halfway through the Ridged Line. A name spoken by many of its denizens, other than Fahroway, is the Darklands. The skies here are completely empty, yet pitch black. There is still seeming light, all things still easy to see, but the skies are black. The grasses of these lands are a vibrant burnt orange, stark against the darkness. The closer the water flows inland, the more it is said to glow a soft turquoise blue.

To the west and central portions of the Darklands, the region grows rocky and full of cliffs, difficult to traverse, but manageable if one knows the way. To the southern end, it flattens out, becoming rolling hills and dense forests, though it frays off into a myriad of peninsulas. The northern end of Fahroway is stepped in absurdity and changes so rapid that it can barely be called a land at all.

To the north, the home of The Flaying Courts presides. To the west, the Yellow Institut calls home. To the south-east, the unfortunates and beasts of yore.

Stoneskin Gaunts

In the middle of the continent of Fahroway, there exists several mountain ranges, though none of those mountains are true piles of stone and earth. They are, instead, collections of various things so large that they create mountains. Anything even remotely valuable or potentially desirable can be found here, though it is not unguarded. The denizens that roam these mountains, called Stoneskin Gaunts, are great gargoyles. They wish only to collect anything of anything that could be desired, though they do not create these things. They create raiding parties to go forth and kill, maim, and destroy so that they might take these things. Should one come across the mountains of the Stoneskin Gaunts, they shall not live long, for the great gargoyles will destroy them for fear that that person might take what is theirs.

Yaets

Yaets are a kingdom of some note, taking only a mere seven-score-and-ten thousand leagues across, located to the north-east of the Yellow Institut and to the east of the Dead Forest, though it’s dwarfed by the territory of the Stoneskin Gaunts and barely even a thought to the Yellow Institut. Little is known about them, due to their insignificance, but they are always seen in giant suits of armor that no human man should be able to bear. They are seen flying various colored banners, but all include green, perhaps some sort of holy color to the Yaets. They have been seen, or, rather, their “civilians” have been seen. Tall beings with strange, twin elongated faces, and oddly bulky.

Despite their great suits of armor, they do not primarily fight face-to-face, instead preferring to use extremely crude devices of great fire and explosive sound, calling them “matchlocks”. For a race of beings so strange, so physically fit, so advantageous, they would do all but face their enemies face-to-face.

They primarily sow and harvest rocruit, stem wheat, and beetroots, though whatever strains they feed upon are stale-tasting but long lasting, taking far longer to rot than most crops of other kingdoms.

Jors

Jors is a simplistic titan among the Tyrisaz, taking glee and joy in endlessly battling with the daemons of the Darklands. He wields a Danskeggox axe, named after Danskeg, the first mortal giant to have created such a tool, and a large, circular, wood-and-hide shield, engraved with runes of various types. His shield is carried by a large, bulky left arm and his axe, a weapon meant to be two-handed, is carried by two right arms, both leaner than the bulky left. His skin has long-since become reddened with bloodflow and adrenaline, a drastic deviation from the snow-white it once was. His beard is chopped and haggard, somehow still reaching below to his rounded beer belly, having been slashed and pulled at by various enemies.

He cares not when beings scratch at him, for runes form where scratches and injuries once were, and any sense of self-preservation have long-since left him after Musanshi gifted him with endless battle-joy, caring not that he bleeds so long as his axe is swinging and his shield is bashing.

It is said that his limited interactions with giant-folk, distant relatives of his, gave to them the name “Thors”, though they likely misunderstood his true name.

Locations of Note

Bellkeep

The Bellkeep is a great tower in central-west Fahroway, standing there since before the Darklands were renamed Fahroway by a race long-dead. The Bellkeep is difficult to get to, hidden away atop the cliffs above the sea, entrapped on one side by the Obsidian Plains not but two-hundred-and-fifty leagues away on the north side and a piece of the Dead Forest to the south, a ways away from the scorched lands between the Yellow Institut and all else.

The tower itself is tall and thick, indeed, but it is a dull gray and rather dull and dusty to look at, the outside of which still covered in webbing from the creatures that crawl and skitter in the Dead Forest, though none of the insides are webbed. It was once a great bell-tower, long faded, and now occupied by a being of strange bird-like quality. The size of it is larger than four, perhaps five great lions, the feathers being a stark white - or perhaps once were, for they are faded now - and the wings are accompanied by several arms. It slowly crawls about inside the Bellkeep, fashioning more bells, guarding it, engraving small enchantments of little or meaningless note upon the outer edges of the bells. Not much is known about its motives, however, for it ate the last visitor who braved the Dead Forest to learn about it.

Darklands Bestiary

As above in Mothua, so below in the Darklands, the creatures here are vast, yet wrong. These are they.

Outer Mimics

Outer Mimics are, most likely, the primary reason that humanity was given the uncanny valley. They are humanoid, but their faces are too stretched and bony, as though the skin is pulled taut over bone. The juveniles are too wrong to be confused for people, but they are flexible, able to hide in the furniture and devices of men in their homes, waiting. They typically target homes on the outer edges of society.

The adults, however, called True Mimics, gain a more human-like appearance, though still visibly wrong. However, they have grown intelligent enough to wear the clothing of the fallen, or, rather, pieces of it, to better aid in their disguise. These ones typically target travellers, Outer Mimics caught in the open, or small outposts.

Krashq'kanov
Resurrected Sunborne

The Resurrected Sunborne are a kingdom legion of skeletal beings in central Krashq’kanov, just to the south of the Obsidian Plains. They are a minor kingdom, seeking to turn all they come across and to reduce them to their own skeletal forms as an expression of ultimate freedom. Freedom from death, freedom from pain, freedom from hunger, and freedom from sickness.

They accomplish this by stringing up these unfortunates and hoisting them high into the air underneath large, special towers called Solagrad.

Solagrad are large, metal towers, purposefully built higher than any other of their buildings in any given town or city, with the top portions having several metal beams sticking out in bizarre formation. At the very top is a large ring, encompassing the perimeter of the tower, followed by another even larger ring round about it, curved, with a specialized glass made to magnify and focus incoming light and heat from the outside, aimed at the beams below. The unfortunates are strung up on the beams and are left there until they themselves become sun-bleached skeletons. Once this is done, they are taken inside and are carefully resurrected and inducted into the legion of undead. The process as a whole may take one, perhaps two years.

In war, the Resurrected Sunborne are relatively easy to manage so long as one can block them out, for their bones are too frail to do much without muscle. All that is needed is to be on a wall and drop large rocks upon them. However, this only works defensively, and they become notably more difficult to advance on, which is why they have never lost ground. When stark red banners bearing a sun-shrouded skull come marching on the horizon, a long, tedious war is due.

Sarbyvich

Sarbyvich is not a person, but a race of predatory arachnae-beings found scattered all around Krashq’kanov, though mostly in southern and western areas. A few, however, can be found in northern West Mothua near Ephernaum.

These beings inhabit “abandoned” houses, though it is more likely that they consumed the inhabitants of those homes long ago. They are primarily out in the middle of nowhere or on the fringes of villages and the like. Often, many of these homes have a central large hole in them, littered with webs and other such things round about them or surrounding these holes. If one looks closely enough at these entries, spindly legs, bent at odd angles, waiting for something foolish to wander inside or get close enough to be eaten.

Strahzzi

Strahzzi are people who make up a kingdom of unknown name - mostly due to even translators failing to understand it - located in eastern Krashq’kanov. These people are a four armed human-like race, though their kingdom is made of several territories, vast and wide, stretching into central and southern Krashq’kanov, below the Obsidian Plains and a ways away from the Resurrected Sunborne.

It is largely unknown what they are like, though this is because visitors are never welcome for long and their language is incomprehensible. They fly colored banners of green and red, using weapons of odd hook-like halberds, honorless are they - such is the curse of those who fly green banners.

Cloaked Gatekeepers

The Cloaked Gatekeepers preside over the Valley of Gates, a hidden valley located in western Krashq’kanov. The only way to this valley is located in south-east Krashq’kanov, underneath the fourth Seeing Tower north from the Azker River. One must descend the tower until darkness becomes day, and the stones fall away to open skies. Far below will be the Valley of Gates, awaiting you should you continue to descend the rest of the tower.

Here are many “gates” of sorts, all in the forms of faces, some opening to other locations through their eyes, through their mouths, or through their ears. Some, however, are not to be trusted, as they are a special breed of mimic that needs not eat for up to decades at a time, only to bite down once something passes through.

However, aside from being covered in countless gates, this valley is not uninhabited. Among them walk wraith knights, beings cloaked in the finest of wools and silks and linens, colored through red, blue, green, yellow, orange, and so on, decorated often with flowers. Not much can be seen of them, as their cloaks and hoods cover their actual bodies, but armored arms and legs can be seen with their movements, wielding odd blades as they cut down intruders. Neither blade nor arrow seem to affect them, so it is best to find your desired gate and leave as soon as possible.

Hrimskeldvar

Hrimskeldvar, one of the Tyrisaz, a race of ancient titan-folk, wanders the lands of Krashq’kanov rather aimlessly, chasing whatever amuses him in the moment. It was he who gave life to the Strahzzi, his ultimate creations, purely to see if he could - or, so he claims, last he was willing to speak. He fashioned the Strahzzi after himself, as he himself has four arms. In his true form, he towers for leagues into the heavens, and yet, does not touch the heavens nor mountain-peaks.

He gave to humanity, of the lands of Mothua, the gift of Runes and all inherited magicks within, though they went on to warp and twist it in the supposed name of the Father Above All. Wrought with disdain at this slight and use of the name of his Grandfather, Hrimskeldvar traveled instead to Krashq’kanov, following about his whims as he pleased there. In doing so, he abandoned Ilbair, though Ilbair never seemed to care much what the other Tyrisaz thought, for Ilbair was - and perhaps still is - the greatest Rune-Seer among them.

Now, more often than not, Hrimskeldvar takes a much smaller form, being only a score-and-ten-and-seven feet tall, with a great antlered helmet and a snow-mist white cloak, using a great longhaft axe as both walking stick and weapon. It is dangerous to encounter him, as his mercy and wrath are dependent on seemingly too many things. To one traveler, he may provide shelter, food, safety, company and drink. To another, he may freeze, split, or hang them where he found them.

Should the mist and snow grow too great, head south as quickly as possible, towards warmth. Hrimskeldvar may not be far behind, but he will not follow you long.

Jakiisi

Jakiisi are a goblin race, once known as Mortal Giants, having devolved over time, residing in dark, snowy mountain villages. They are nearly a man-and-a-half in height, though they often take crouched positions for the purposes of stealth, as they frequently hunt game for food. Their skin is generally a cold, light grey, often bony, with chunky white hair. Their hands, forearms, legs, and feet are rather large, though almost purely muscle, and they are largely clothed in grays and whites to better blend into the dark mist and snow.

They have a more tribalistic society, scattered amongst the mountain ranges of Krashq’kanov, though mostly north of the Obsidian Plains, ruled by chiefs. Each chief becomes one after a trial-by-combat, where the former chief left for the Ættestup to preserve his honor.

Their weaponry is generally sharp and jagged, though mostly using spears, shepherd’s axes, and long bows with stony tips that can be swung to hurt more immediate enemies. They have a scratchy language as well, which appears tied to their magicks, using watered down versions of runes and incantations, incantations being spells that require chanting of sorts, used by Hos-Jakiisi.

Mothua

Mothua is divided primarily into three larger land masses. One mass to the west, one to the north-east, connected by two isthmuses and nearly connected at another point by two small peninsulas on either side, connected now by large bridges. Between the central connection between the two land masses in the southern end is another isthmus, rather large, with a two week journey on foot from one end to another in the thinnest section, connecting both West Mothua and North-East Mothua to South Mothua. This isthmus is called the Spiced Isthmus.

The first and north-most isthmus, connecting West and North-East Mothua, is called the Verdant-Hewn Isthmus. The second and lower isthmus is referred to as the Pentahl Isthmus.

Western and North-Eastern Mothua

Both Western and North-Eastern Mothua are largely similar in terms of the lands within them. There are five major forests throughout the two, each referred to as an Ancient Wood, where the forest grows thick and the sun grows dark, blotted out by their canopies. They are located in the northern end of North-East Mothua, the south western end of North-East Mothua, the middle east section of West Mothua, the north-western section of West Mothua, and slightly west of the Spiced Isthmus. There are more normal forests surrounding them, though the northern Ancient Wood in North-East Mothua directly faces the coastline and beaches there.

There are two mountain ranges through them. The western range starts from the north, near the city of Hesterbaum, going all the way down to the southern end of West Mothua, moved slightly to the west. Near halfway down, it branches off into another set of mountains reaching all the way to the Verdant-Hewn Isthmus, stopping just forty-three miles before it. The second range goes from the lower end of North-East Mothua, stretching west, across just above three hundred miles, ending with two mountains encircling the Dipping Lake.

Spiced Isthmus

The spiced Isthmus is made, primarily, of the same ever-blue sands of the Far-Dunes, otherwise known as South Mothua. The lands here consist of a few dunes here and there, but they are primarily flat, broken up only by a singular city, the Red City, on the eastern coast of the southern end of the isthmus.

Southern Mothua

South Mothua is largely an all around singular biome, made of a blue-sanded desert called the Far-Dunes. The coast-line ranges anywhere from sea-level to sheer cliffs high above the waves below. The vast majority of the Far-Dunes are flat and barren, but near the central sections and southern, south-eastern, and eastern sections they begin forming great dunes of vibrant blue hue. The sand itself produces fumes, similar to odd and unknown spices, that frequently irritate the eyes and sense of smell of humans and most normal creatures, such as livestock. The lands here are hot, but, as Mothua as a whole is above the Ridged Line (a series of tall rocks in the oceans nearby), it is not as hot as it could otherwise be. There are few oases are ringed with a few odd trees and a water of dark gray hue, unsafe to drink without a filter of some sort.

Noteworthy Locations

Redborne

Redborne is a strange land, located in North-East Mothua between the Verdant-Hewn and the Pentahl isthmuses. The grasses here, as one could imagine, are of a brilliant red, though the sky has shifted to become permanently black in this location, said to be the scorch-marks across the heavens, left behind by a god of fire before he was slain by his kin. There are few features in these lands - rivers still blue, flowing from the great lakes between the isthmuses - but there are no mountains, no forests, no deserts, nothing. All that there is are a few scattered large white buildings of stone blocks. Residing in these are a people of paper-white skin, heads like spheres, who slowly meander about, tilling the red grassed soil. Their clothes are mere black drapes, tatters, even, but there is enough to cover all skin below the throat. They seemingly do not respond to any outside intervention, they have never been seen moving faster than a slow shuffle, and their heads are always downturned towards the soil. As they do little, and prevent nothing from happening to them, the Anointed Holy Cities will occasionally send a party to retrieve food from them. However, the people here do not seem to mind, or even notice, that their food is being stolen - they have never been seen eating at all.

Dunes of Endless Visage

In the southern end of central South Mothua, just north of the coastal cliff-faces, are literal faces peering from the sand. They are unmoving, eyes and mouths closed, made of a red colored stone. Each is the size of a few carriages at least, though none of them have ever been exactly measured. The faces themselves are perfectly neutral, almost sleeping in expression, but nothing happens no matter how much one might dance or stomp about on the faces. While it is unknown where they came from or why they are there, it is best not to linger.

Meandering Crosslands

The Meandering Crosslands are a giant, seemingly endless - though not actually so - field of skulls, crosses, and various weapons stabbed into the ground. Located just to the north-east of the Spiced Isthmus, it is the remains of a battlefield of a score of thousands of years long past. The water here is drinkable, found only in small streams, but it fills the drinker with a sense of emptiness. The closer to the Spiced Isthmus the streams are, the worse they are to drink, as they flow in from the sea there and begin to become saltwater.

Ribbed Gates

The Ribbed Gates are a pair of megastructures, each made of four giant pillars. The four pillars each reach straight upwards, connected by a myriad of white, blue, and yellow marble beams with a completely flat roof of the same substance over it. Between each pillar is an archway with a giant ribcage over it, reaching from pillar to pillar, on each face. The Ribbed Gates are located at the land bridge between West Mothua and North-East Mothua. They are so large that ranks of two score and ten across can march through without their shoulders touching the pillars.

The Yellow Manse

The Yellow Manse is found in central North-East Mothua, just a few miles south-west of the small town of Starrkhalta, a small lumbermill town. Deeper into the woods is a divide that slowly goes a little deeper, until the ground is near two-head height, before opening up into a clearing with overgrown trees and vines round about. In the center of this clearing is a large home, tall and thin, ranging from a deep, sunburnt orange to a sickly yellow as it rises higher across the building. Steep black outcrops and rooflets are scattered across the building, hanging over ornate blue and green stained glass windows. It is said to be home of Hinter-Witch Hilda, a small thin woman of platinum blond hair, lemon-green blind eyes, and a navy blue heavily patterned cloak. Whisps and little flits of smoke and skulls float from round about her as she walks, a slow and leisurely pace, as all Hinter-Witches. Not much is known about her, as the townsfolk don’t like to visit her much, but when a wound is too great, when a disaster is too much, Hinter-Witch Hilda is the one to call upon - though always for a price.

Utmar’s Bridge

Utmar’s Bridge is not one singular location, but a range of locations across the entirety of West Mothua and North-East Mothua. Large red-stone and orange-stone pillars, adorned and gilded in black skeletons, jut upwards high into the air. Atop these pillars is a large white stone waterway with a three-score foot distance across, either side with a large and wide platform. The waterway, stained gray from the collected water round about and from the sea all mixing together, is shallow but deep enough to carry cargo ships.

The most common type of ship found is a Great Carrier, a long, wide barge with thin but durable slabs of wood reaching upwards at the sides. Connected to each of these pillars is a long chain-pole, used to help pull the Great Carriers along by Grifmoore bulls hooked up to carefully made harnesses. It’s the safest and fastest method to transport large amounts of precious cargo across West Mothua and North-West Mothua between the Anointed Holy Cities, various castles, forts, and towns. At each major location, there is an elaborate water-crane system to raise or lower the barges, while at major intersections, the Utmar’s Bridges turn into a large ring or into a small raised lake to carefully get around one another.

Giant’s Dip

The Giant’s Dip is a large, lake-sized downwards dip into the ground in central West Mothua, still overgrown with plantlife, going down shallowly. At the bottom is a large cave with a tunnel that leads to a few locations - a seaside cove, a hole in the earth of the Great Ring, a hole in the ground in an Ancient Wood, and a large underground reservoir of water.

The Great Ring

Around seven of the thirteen Anointed Holy Cities is a massive drop into the earth. Far, far below, beyond the common man’s eyesight, the mountain cities vanish. The Great Ring is just the stony cliff-faces ringed round about the Anointed Holy Cities, though Utmar’s Bridge reaches across at several different points. Around each point, where a water-crane port lies, is a small city. It’s a singular city, spread across all of them - it’s thin and not at all tall, but it’s a series of black and verdant green block-like buildings with white vines and gothic-styled roofs atop each. The earth here has reddened a little from an unknown number of millennia ago, and the grasslands have turned sun-burnt orange. This city, Fechlind, is surrounded by fields of vibrant crimson wheat and several farmlands.

Hesterbaum

Hesterbaum was once an Anointed Holy City, one that would make the total rise to fourteen cities if it were still present. It was located in the south-western end of West Mothua, surrounded and built around several stone and marble quarries, The inhabitants of the city, seemingly overnight ten-and-six thousand years prior, vanished without a trace, leaving the city completely empty. The constant travel and quarries pushed the plantlife around the city away, a thing that has never recovered, and the city, now no longer maintained, has long since become sun-bleached and ruined, losing all color and splendor it might have once had. This act of vanishing, along with the kingdom ruled by the Hinter-Witches appearing just to the east, triggered a series of Holy Crusades called the War of the Abhorrents. It is commonly believed by the Inner Sanctum that the Hinter-Witches likely did not cause the vanishing, but the Hinter-Witches were a fine scape-goat to expand the rule of the Anointed Holy Cities, just as the One Faith wishes.

Important Cities

Ephernaum

Ephernaum is a merchant city belonging to the Anointed Holy Cities located in the central northern end of West Mothua, controlled largely by Great House Arkithane and several smaller noble houses. It is built atop a series of flattened mountains. Ringed about the city are several fertile steppes that lower ever so slightly with a grand wall of gray and red build at each boundary, acting as layered defense. Ephernaum is useful for, and has been used for, staging attacks and Holy Crusades out into North-East Mothua and across the northern seas towards Krashq’kanov.

Hesterbaum

Leviathas

Leviathas is an Anointed Holy City in the far western coasts of West Mothua, leaning towards the northern end of the sub-continent. The city is build atop several cavernous mountains and several cliff faces, many of which have been carved into for housing and various other buildings. It is home to the Abyssal Cults, they who refer to themselves as Jegari.

The Red City

The Red City is located in the Spiced Isthmus, between West, North-East, and South Mothua. It’s a trading hotspot, having a myriad of spices, exotic materials, and providing a comparatively safe trading port between Mothua and nearby continents or islands. However, as safe as it is, it is wise to return to one’s inn or home when the sun goes down.

Great Rim Cities

The Great Rim Cities are not one city, but seven, located in the supermassive pit of the Great Ring. Their names are often unimportant, as the cities have long since melded together. Rot, pestilence, and strange stirrings of daemon’s wind flow down in the dark depths of the Great Ring while the peaks of the cities reach the heavens. They are a mass of color and splendor, adorned with white, gray, or black gothic detailing, elaborate and colorful stained glass, and a number of monuments untold. It could take a score of a hundred lifetimes to explore every inch of the Great Rim Cities, though by then, much would have changed and all progress would have been cast to the way-side.

Spindle Knights

The Spindle Knights are a small order of, what the Anointed Holy Cities call, Abhorrent Humans. They are unusually tall and spindly, hence their self-proclaimed name of the Spindle Knights. They are covered head-to-toe in a thin metal armor, fashioned after the armor of various Noble Houses of the Anointed Holy Cities’ Nobility. The quality of the armor, however, is rather dubious, and the coloration is a green-hued silver. They each wield an abnormally long halberd, however, which appears to be rather effective for surprisingly long forwards stabbing motions and for use in cleaving, like using a bladed staff.

They reside within the small valley found in the southern end of West Mothua, just a few miles off the coast there. It is a heavily wooded area, though the trees are thin, tall, and lush with bright greenery and yellows, abundant with sunlight. The Spindle Knights themselves claim to be chivalrous, but rarely ever do anything of note, preferring to stay within their little valley home. They are, in addition, so inactive that not even the Anointed Holy Cities cares about their presence or existence.

Taleuri

The Taleuri reside on the Spiced Isthmus, a mass of land that connects West Mothua, North-East Mothua, and South Mothua. Near the northern and western edges of the Spiced Isthmus, the usual plantlife and vegetation of the northern half of Mothua quickly disperses into the vibrant blue sands of South Mothua.

These people, the Taleuri, are small figures dressed in brown and red heavy robes, though their purple-hued skin can be seen between the robes. They operate the Galleosands, large galleon-like ships made to skim across the surface of the blue sands below. They ferry various peoples and cargos from one end of Spiced Isthmus to the other, stopping every now and then in the Red City on the eastern coast of the southern end of Spiced Isthmus. If payment cannot be made in coin or valuable items, payment will be made in flesh and bone.

Those Who Watch

Those Who Watch are a handful, a score, maybe two score, of tall, bulky, skeletal figures. Their skeletons are similar to that of a human skeleton, but there are oddities - certain bones are too thick, some are melded together, there are bones where they should not be, and certain bones are far too elongated to be human, like the thick cord of bones reaching from their spine to the base of their skulls, nearly two feet in distance.

They wear large, white cloaks with silver pauldrons atop the shoulders of the cloak, held together by a simple silver clasp. In their right hand, they carry a weapon. The handle is the length of a standard male Shrike’s entire arm, the pommel a rounded metal ball, and the guard is completely missing. The blade itself is far too large to be called a sword, it is more akin to a massive slab of sharpened marble. It has a single edge, with the blunt half adorned with a skull where the guard should be and skeletal hands and ribcages reaching up the length of the blade.

Those Who Watch slowly wander the Meandering Crosslands. They are silent, never speaking, to the extent that their lower jaw is completely missing. They do little, and will remain doing little, so long as one does not disturb the crosses or bones of those fallen long ago.

Hinter-Witches

The Hinter-Witches are an old order, with the earliest recordings of them being seen before the vanishing of the people of Hesterbaum. They were not seen around there, merely that they were earliest recorded then.

At one time, they attempted to rule West Mothua, though after many Holy Crusades and burnings at the stakes, what was left of the Hinter-Witches were scattered across the eastern edges of West Mothua and across North-East Mothua. There are only a few of them left that are known at present time, reduced to strange women in odd places, each doing as they see fit for the rest of their seemingly endless lives.

Ilbair

Ilbair is a being known as a Tyrisaz, a race of titan-folk, rare and far apart. Ilbair is, visibly, deformed, possessing no legs and an odd pelvis of sorts, though his entire form is skeletal, marking him as strange even among the Tyrisaz. His inner bones are ashen black, yet his exterior are a bright silver-like bone, though somehow without shine and yet remaining silver. Attached to his shoulders are each three arms, though the left shoulder has the beginnings of a fourth arm that seems to have been crushed or torn off and apart long ago.

Ilbair sits in south-central North-East Mothua, just staying near the coast. Rarely does he move, except to take a colossal drag of a strange, gargantuan cigar of sorts. However, on the few occasions that Ilbair has been angered, he has taken a drag before spewing a sea of orange-green flame, the likes of which has consumed the armies of the Fourth Holy Crusade.

Ilbair does not speak, nor does he leave his place, but he does not take kindly to those who disturb his peace.

Ekternau

Ekternau, otherwise known as the Misty Islands, are a home of Dredgefolk.

There are only a handful of islands, each large, but splintered, leaving a myriad of waterways through each, various coves and bays, lakes, and so on. The islands themselves are coated in a thick mist. Each island themselves are not named, only the cities built on them.

Grenway

Grenway, located on the east-most island, is a relatively standard city for Ekternau, built high into the misty air atop razor thin buildings and stilts. No building is separate, each stacked atop one another precariously and connected loosely by thin, rotten pathways and rope bridges. However, unlike the other cities of Ekternau, it has fallen long ago, falling specifically to a Dredgkrak, a great, kraken-like beast that inhabits ruins. Obscured by the mist, various tendrils and tentacles of this creature slowly wind through and around the massive towers and buildings. Many buildings have long-since been hollowed out, holding smaller spawn of this creature, waiting to feed on each other and those unlucky enough to come across them.

Jentrybol

Jentrybol is another stilted city, obscured by the mist, located on the north-eastern island of Ekternau, the island closest to South Mothua. Here, the Dredgefolk are quiet, solemn, and mean, prioritizing work above all else. In the midst of their city is a large, monument tower, near five times as thick as the next thickest tower-buildings in the city. Carved into the sides, on all four, are large solemn men, each with an anchorage resting calmly in their hands with the heads of which resting at the bottom of the tower beneath the water’s surface far below. A myriad of thick chains connects the corners of this tower to various nearby buildings, helping to keep them upright. Large nets and ropes can be seen on the lower edges, vanishing into the mist below, only to pull up large nets and buckets of salt-sick fish.

Rozwal

This is the only other city of any important note, as all the others are nearly alike each other. Rozwal is located on the north-western island, though it has walkways clear down from the towers to the ocean below, a bit away from the actual island itself despite towering over that as well. Here, along long boardwalks, are ships and vessels so large that they cannot be seen in their entirety within the mist. These ships, too tall to see and longer than the eye can fathom, slowly sail to and fro among the islands and nearby continents, trading fish, salt, and parts of various sea creatures and leviathans.

Fourthfog Bestiary

The Fourthfog Bestiary is a large book, a collection of known creatures residing on the islands of Ekternau. This is just a collection of some creatures that can be found, though not all, as not everything has been documented successfully. It is called the Fourthfog Bestiary for a handful of small reasons, the first being that it is first and foremost a bestiary. The others, fog, for being the bestiary of the foggy islands of Ekternau, and fourth for being the fourth attempt at such a thing. There was once a fifth being produced, though the writer and explorer of it was eaten by a Chrant Mimic before he could finish it.

Ylraz Frog

The Ylraz Frog resides on the island which Jentrybol is located, though they can be found on a few other islands. They look like a strange mix of frog and lilypad, able to blend in as lilypads, which they can take the form of for extended periods of time due to their powerful little lungs. These creatures often will fire their tongues deep into the water, which they focus into a spear-like shape to impale saltfish to bring them up and eat. An Ylraz Frog can cause minor wounds to a person, though it is unwise to sleep near places of their habitats due to the fact that their tongues can pierce the throat. Additionally, it is unwise to eat them, as they are poisonous due to the deadwater being soaked into their skin.

Chrant Mimic

The Chrant Mimic, named after the first person who was eaten by these creatures, Edar Chrant, is a Mimic-type creature. They assume the form of branches of various trees, skin colored to match the tree they attach to. The end of the “branches” split, often, into three or four jaws with a coiled, grabbing tongue, meant to snare any passerby or passing creature to be consumed. However, Chrant Mimics have been seen consuming other Chrant Mimics that have failed to blend in or that are on the move. The Chrant Mimics can be found in the southern islands of Ekternau.

Sedyr

Sedyrs are fanciful, coral-like, serpent-like creatures with small limbs, clawed and webbed little arms in several places round about. They are rather large, the juveniles reaching crossing end-to-end near a score-and-ten feet long. These creatures are proud, territorial creatures, and have often been seen writhing and biting at each other, able to spit out highly pressurized streams of water sharp enough to begin cutting through stone - though it would take a moment to fully cut through. They don’t actually reside on the islands themselves, but rather, in the water pools, rivers, and the coasts. It’s advised to take scorched-metal shields when dealing with them, wait for one to deal with another, or wait for those of the Ekternau Enclave to deal with one and leave part of the carcass behind.

Stiltleg Elk

The Stiltleg Elk are tall, elk-like creatures, though their bodies are a bit smaller. Their legs are tall and oddly thin, having four front legs and two hind legs, allowing them to technically tower over normal elk. Their antlers are not spread out so much to the sides, instead hanging forwards. Their fur is a foggy-green, meant to blend in well to the mist, and their legs and clawed hooves are a bark-black. Stiltleg Elks are fast and quick, surprisingly agile and dextrous, able to skitter over rocks in ponds and lakes, across rivers and streams with ease. They’re more passive, but aggressive if one gets too close. They’re not a good source of meat, due to their thinner, smaller bodies, but they’re better than nothing. Additionally, the clawed hooves aren’t much more dangerous than normal hooves, unable to flex really or grab, only meant to stamp over and hook into various things for better grip.

Stonefisk

Stonefisks are lethal little critters, disguised as small stones, barely the size of twice a fist. Spread apart and dissected, they appear like a mix of crustacean and arachnid, though they have a rocky shell. If their rocky shell is stepped on, they stab a stinger up and into their prey, poisoning them, before the Stonefisk begins to skitter along - at a distance - after the prey. Once said creature falls, the Stonefisk eats its fill and will then hide nearby if the carcass hasn’t been finished off, waiting to eat until later when it hungers again.

Iystrobahths

“Iystrobahths is a Lesser God, a hidden being of great malefic delight, descended far and away from their mother, Musanshi, albeit distantly. It is a forgotten and abandoned being, but it cares little for these facts, seeking instead purely to torment. It can be found, with extreme difficulty, roaming the thickest fog-ridden islands, though it is believed that it primarily resides in the central-most island of Ekternau.

Iystrobahths is giant, nigh the size of a score-and-nine human men at full height, though it is more elklike, having the head of a stiltleg elk. In place of the two eyes that the standard stiltleg elk possesses, it has seven eyes, all a blind, milky blue, with one large eye on the forehead being a milky yellow permanently leaking odd green tears. In place of legs, it has a myriad of arms and giant hands holding it up, so much so that the central body cannot be seen from beneath. Additionally, six freakishly long, thin arms are just below the jaw, made for precision, that can grab a man and fiddle with the smallest details of he.

As said by the last sent to speak with it, Iystrobahths has several antlers, though there are a myriad of antlers around a long, thick fog-blue shag-fur, though more specifically concentrated at the tip of the tail. However, the traveller couldn’t recall much else, nor could he be sent back to see, for Iystrobahths plucked his eyes and consumed them as payment to be allowed to live.

It is said that Iystrobahths was the one who introduced the Dredgkrak to Grenway, leading to the deaths of those who resides there, likely for its own amusement.

Great Seas

The Great Seas appear vast and empty at first, but only a fool would believe such a thing. Below the surface, great fish, whales, krakens, leviathans, and greater leviathans await. In the fogs and clouds, beings of various sorts descend to consume. This is but a small list of these creatures.

Rozjalczy

Rozjalczy are a small kraken-like leviathan. Their forebodies are those of a massive shark, possessing an upper midnight-purple coloration and a lower stark white coloration. Their eyes are an empty, unfathomable orange-yellow. This forebody is a little smaller than a galleon, while their end does not descend into a proper fin. Instead, it descends into a myriad of kraken-like tendrils, used to grab onto prey and ships to subdue them for prompt consumption, using hook-like beaks along either side of these tendrils to dig into prey and ship alike. Due to the coloration of their upper forebodies and tendrils, Rozjalczy are extremely valuable leviathans to hunt. Additionally, much of their sinew can be used for high-quality greases for fire pots used by catapults. Rozjalczy typically swim in colder waters, typically found in the northern portions of Yggrdziel and underneath the Screaming Moon.

Pale Worm

The Pale Worm is one of the smallest leviathan-whale hybrids, though, despite being a leviathan-whale, it is often left alone due to the lack of anything valuable compared to the difficulties provided when catching one. They are only a couple hundred feet long with a mouth large enough to consume just shy of a dozen men. Inside is no tongue, only teeth lining the insides. There are two black voids where eyes should be, with the skin rucked up round about as though the eyes were plucked out long ago. There isn’t much by way of any insides for valuables, but they are surprisingly fast and are extremely aggressive. Rest assured, if you see one, it has already seen you.

Fogworn Glaucus

The Fogworn Glaucus is a drastically larger version of the glaucus atlanticus, nearly the size of two galleons. Instead of the traditional blacks and blues, it is colored with light grays and misty greens, meant to blend into the fog, clouds, and seas that it inhabits. It rests high within the clouds, though none quiet know how, and it primarily glides about. Its face is adorned with two smooth, extremely forwards leaning horns, and in place of a mouth are dozens of mandibles. It feasts primarily on great fish and the carcasses of whales and leviathans that have floated too high to the surface. It’s passive, for the most part, but aggressive when in the fog. The Fogworn Glaucus is worthwhile to hunt due to it’s hide and hollow bones, as the hide is tough yet light, and the bones make for excellent instruments and tools of various kinds. From the horns, it is able to gather moisture and heat in the air and, using an internal organ useful for chemical weapons, it’s able to charge up and let out a pale green bolt of lightning for hunting and territory defense purposes.

False Galleon

The False Galleon is a small kraken of sorts that calls the seas beneath the Screaming Moon home. It inhabits abandoned or shipwrecked galleons, bringing them to the surface and “sailing” them, though it only uses this to safely get closer to other ships or to lure great fish to it before lunging at and attempting to consume its prey. From afar, it is difficult to tell, but there will be signs that something is amiss.

Qasirpran

The Qasirpran is a great “fish” in the vague shape of a shrimp. It is black with a ghostly teal along the top, a being of the depths, whose innards glow. Upon opening its maw, a baleful light will shine forth, blinding in the darkness of the deep, something that makes the rest of the creature nigh on impossible to see. This light often lures in other great fish and whatever may lurk, though upon getting close enough, it is easy to tell what the source of the light is. However, the Qasirpran possesses extreme burst-swimming speed, unsustainable for long periods of time, and can extend four “inner” jaws, each layered within another, to reach and bite. It calls the depths of the cliffs of southern Fahroway home and the depths three-score thousand leagues north-west of Ekternau home.
