Locations of Note

Fahroway

Bellkeep

The Bellkeep is a great tower in central-west Fahroway, standing there since before the Darklands were renamed Fahroway by a race long-dead. The Bellkeep is difficult to get to, hidden away atop the cliffs above the sea, entrapped on one side by the Obsidian Plains not but two-hundred-and-fifty leagues away on the north side and a piece of the Dead Forest to the south, a ways away from the scorched lands between the Yellow Institut and all else.

The tower itself is tall and thick, indeed, but it is a dull gray and rather dull and dusty to look at, the outside of which still covered in webbing from the creatures that crawl and skitter in the Dead Forest, though none of the insides are webbed. It was once a great bell-tower, long faded, and now occupied by a being of strange bird-like quality. The size of it is larger than four, perhaps five great lions, the feathers being a stark white - or perhaps once were, for they are faded now - and the wings are accompanied by several arms. It slowly crawls about inside the Bellkeep, fashioning more bells, guarding it, engraving small enchantments of little or meaningless note upon the outer edges of the bells. Not much is known about its motives, however, for it ate the last visitor who braved the Dead Forest to learn about it.

Mothua

Redborne

Redborne is a strange land, located in North-East Mothua between the Verdant-Hewn and the Pentahl isthmuses. The grasses here, as one could imagine, are of a brilliant red, though the sky has shifted to become permanently black in this location, said to be the scorch-marks across the heavens, left behind by a god of fire before he was slain by his kin. There are few features in these lands - rivers still blue, flowing from the great lakes between the isthmuses - but there are no mountains, no forests, no deserts, nothing. All that there is are a few scattered large white buildings of stone blocks. Residing in these are a people of paper-white skin, heads like spheres, who slowly meander about, tilling the red grassed soil. Their clothes are mere black drapes, tatters, even, but there is enough to cover all skin below the throat. They seemingly do not respond to any outside intervention, they have never been seen moving faster than a slow shuffle, and their heads are always downturned towards the soil. As they do little, and prevent nothing from happening to them, the Anointed Holy Cities will occasionally send a party to retrieve food from them. However, the people here do not seem to mind, or even notice, that their food is being stolen - they have never been seen eating at all.

Dunes of Endless Visage

In the southern end of central South Mothua, just north of the coastal cliff-faces, are literal faces peering from the sand. They are unmoving, eyes and mouths closed, made of a red colored stone. Each is the size of a few carriages at least, though none of them have ever been exactly measured. The faces themselves are perfectly neutral, almost sleeping in expression, but nothing happens no matter how much one might dance or stomp about on the faces. While it is unknown where they came from or why they are there, it is best not to linger.

Meandering Crosslands

The Meandering Crosslands are a giant, seemingly endless - though not actually so - field of skulls, crosses, and various weapons stabbed into the ground. Located just to the north-east of the Spiced Isthmus, it is the remains of a battlefield of a score of thousands of years long past. The water here is drinkable, found only in small streams, but it fills the drinker with a sense of emptiness. The closer to the Spiced Isthmus the streams are, the worse they are to drink, as they flow in from the sea there and begin to become saltwater.

Ribbed Gates

The Ribbed Gates are a pair of megastructures, each made of four giant pillars. The four pillars each reach straight upwards, connected by a myriad of white, blue, and yellow marble beams with a completely flat roof of the same substance over it. Between each pillar is an archway with a giant ribcage over it, reaching from pillar to pillar, on each face. The Ribbed Gates are located at the land bridge between West Mothua and North-East Mothua. They are so large that ranks of two score and ten across can march through without their shoulders touching the pillars.

The Yellow Manse

The Yellow Manse is found in central North-East Mothua, just a few miles south-west of the small town of Starrkhalta, a small lumbermill town. Deeper into the woods is a divide that slowly goes a little deeper, until the ground is near two-head height, before opening up into a clearing with overgrown trees and vines round about. In the center of this clearing is a large home, tall and thin, ranging from a deep, sunburnt orange to a sickly yellow as it rises higher across the building. Steep black outcrops and rooflets are scattered across the building, hanging over ornate blue and green stained glass windows. It is said to be home of Hinter-Witch Hilda, a small thin woman of platinum blond hair, lemon-green blind eyes, and a navy blue heavily patterned cloak. Whisps and little flits of smoke and skulls float from round about her as she walks, a slow and leisurely pace, as all Hinter-Witches. Not much is known about her, as the townsfolk don’t like to visit her much, but when a wound is too great, when a disaster is too much, Hinter-Witch Hilda is the one to call upon - though always for a price.

Utmar’s Bridge

Utmar’s Bridge is not one singular location, but a range of locations across the entirety of West Mothua and North-East Mothua. Large red-stone and orange-stone pillars, adorned and gilded in black skeletons, jut upwards high into the air. Atop these pillars is a large white stone waterway with a three-score foot distance across, either side with a large and wide platform. The waterway, stained gray from the collected water round about and from the sea all mixing together, is shallow but deep enough to carry cargo ships.

The most common type of ship found is a Great Carrier, a long, wide barge with thin but durable slabs of wood reaching upwards at the sides. Connected to each of these pillars is a long chain-pole, used to help pull the Great Carriers along by Grifmoore bulls hooked up to carefully made harnesses. It’s the safest and fastest method to transport large amounts of precious cargo across West Mothua and North-East Mothua between the Anointed Holy Cities, various castles, forts, and towns. At each major location, there is an elaborate water-crane system to raise or lower the barges, while at major intersections, the Utmar’s Bridges turn into a large ring or into a small raised lake to carefully get around one another.

Giant’s Dip

The Giant’s Dip is a large, lake-sized downwards dip into the ground in central West Mothua, still overgrown with plantlife, going down shallowly. At the bottom is a large cave with a tunnel that leads to a few locations - a seaside cove, a hole in the earth of the Great Ring, a hole in the ground in an Ancient Wood, and a large underground reservoir of water.

The Great Ring

Around seven of the thirteen Anointed Holy Cities is a massive drop into the earth. Far, far below, beyond the common man’s eyesight, the mountain cities vanish. The Great Ring is just the stony cliff-faces ringed round about the Anointed Holy Cities, though Utmar’s Bridge reaches across at several different points. Around each point, where a water-crane port lies, is a small city. It’s a singular city, spread across all of them - it’s thin and not at all tall, but it’s a series of black and verdant green block-like buildings with white vines and gothic-styled roofs atop each. The earth here has reddened a little from an unknown number of millennia ago, and the grasslands have turned sun-burnt orange. This city, Fechlind, is surrounded by fields of vibrant crimson wheat and several farmlands.

Hesterbaum

Hesterbaum was once an Anointed Holy City, one that would make the total rise to fourteen cities if it were still present. It was located in the south-western end of West Mothua, surrounded and built around several stone and marble quarries, The inhabitants of the city, seemingly overnight ten-and-six thousand years prior, vanished without a trace, leaving the city completely empty. The constant travel and quarries pushed the plantlife around the city away, a thing that has never recovered, and the city, now no longer maintained, has long since become sun-bleached and ruined, losing all color and splendor it might have once had. This act of vanishing, along with the kingdom ruled by the Hinter-Witches appearing just to the east, triggered a series of Holy Crusades called the War of the Abhorrents. It is commonly believed by the Inner Sanctum that the Hinter-Witches likely did not cause the vanishing, but the Hinter-Witches were a fine scape-goat to expand the rule of the Anointed Holy Cities, just as the One Faith wishes.

Ekternau

Ekternau, otherwise known as the Misty Islands, are a home of Dredgefolk.

There are only a handful of islands, each large, but splintered, leaving a myriad of waterways through each, various coves and bays, lakes, and so on. The islands themselves are coated in a thick mist. Each island themselves are not named, only the cities built on them.

Grenway

Grenway, located on the east-most island, is a relatively standard city for Ekternau, built high into the misty air atop razor thin buildings and stilts. No building is separate, each stacked atop one another precariously and connected loosely by thin, rotten pathways and rope bridges. However, unlike the other cities of Ekternau, it has fallen long ago, falling specifically to a Dredgkrak, a great, kraken-like beast that inhabits ruins. Obscured by the mist, various tendrils and tentacles of this creature slowly wind through and around the massive towers and buildings. Many buildings have long-since been hollowed out, holding smaller spawn of this creature, waiting to feed on each other and those unlucky enough to come across them.

Jentrybol

Jentrybol is another stilted city, obscured by the mist, located on the north-eastern island of Ekternau, the island closest to South Mothua. Here, the Dredgefolk are quiet, solemn, and mean, prioritizing work above all else. In the midst of their city is a large, monument tower, near five times as thick as the next thickest tower-buildings in the city. Carved into the sides, on all four, are large solemn men, each with an anchorage resting calmly in their hands with the heads of which resting at the bottom of the tower beneath the water’s surface far below. A myriad of thick chains connects the corners of this tower to various nearby buildings, helping to keep them upright. Large nets and ropes can be seen on the lower edges, vanishing into the mist below, only to pull up large nets and buckets of salt-sick fish.

Rozwal

This is the only other city of any important note, as all the others are nearly alike each other. Rozwal is located on the north-western island, though it has walkways clear down from the towers to the ocean below, a bit away from the actual island itself despite towering over that as well. Here, along long boardwalks, are ships and vessels so large that they cannot be seen in their entirety within the mist. These ships, too tall to see and longer than the eye can fathom, slowly sail to and fro among the islands and nearby continents, trading fish, salt, and parts of various sea creatures and leviathans.

Krashq’kanov

White-Moon Palace

Within the Valley of Gates is a white pillared gate with purple foliage growing round about it, leading to an unknown land. This land, dark, yet illuminated, having a sky of a deep and beautiful green, seems to exist solely to contain the palace ruins found there. A plane between planes, perhaps, though such a thing is common within the Valley of Gates.

The palace found there has long-since been abandoned, left empty and crumbled, made purely of white marble and stone with all sorts of purple-leafed and purple-flowered foliage. There is little to be had here, yet, within the throne-room, the ceiling of glass depicts the Screaming Moon, imagery of the Tale of Creation, and much of Waehatl’s known - and some unknown - depictions.

Some have theorized that this place, now called the White-Moon Palace, served as a gateway between the mortal realms and the palace of Waehatl, the Screaming Moon itself.
