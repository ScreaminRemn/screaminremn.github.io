Yellow Institut

The Yellow Institut are but a small sampling of the race of men, hidden away on the far western reaches of the continent of Fahroway. This region does not have beaches like most coastal places do, they instead of sheer cliffs hundreds of feet above the ocean floor. The region is mountainous and riddled with thick forests, cliffs, canyons, valleys, and mountain ranges, only serving to bolster what the Yellow Institut desires above all else: isolation.

They are isolationists through-and-through, having set up heavy defensive outposts, walls, and various forces all along their borders, seeking to drive away any and all who approach with extreme prejudice. They wish to be left alone and, through sheer violence, they intend to have their wish.

Military Doctrine

Most members of the armies of the Yellow Institut wear a half visor sallet with a gorget. Beyond this, nearly every member is entirely different depending on their role. The Yellow Institut favors highly elite and long ranged combat, as well as anything that can deny large amounts of ground.

The infantry of the Yellow Institut are highly elite through highly specialized equipment.

Their armor consists of heavily padded cloth, followed by chainmail, then a quartered yellow and white tabard. They wear a half visor sallet with accompanying metal gorget. They have a pauldron on their left shoulder with a spacer and a small metal plate to act as a small shield. On both arms, they have metal vambraces and mismatched metal gauntlets. Additionally, they have greaves and segmented sabatons. In their right hand, they have a swordstaff, and at their left hip they have a sheathed poignard.

Their left gauntlet is highly specialized and much larger than their right gauntlet. It’s made of tempered steel with various engraved symbols. As a gauntlet, it allows the soldier to wield his swordstaff with both hands, yet as large as it is, it can be used as a small shield. The symbols, when channeled, can create large flares of blinding fire to cast in front of oneself, used at spear-length to blind a target, to burn them, and then to stab through the fire to destroy his enemy.

They fight in a spread formation to cover as much area as possible per soldier and to avoid burning each other.

The Yellow Institut uses multiple types of artillery, as artillery is their main focus and the predominant force within their armies.

First, their archers, who have little metal armor outside of a half visor sallet. They wear heavily padded cloth and quartered white and yellow tabards. At their hip, they have a messer, and at their thigh on their dominant side, they have a seax. Their primary weapon is a large, heavy warbow, used to fire specialized arrows. Each archer carries two quivers with them, the first being for regular arrows, the second for arrows engraved with symbols that detonate on high speed impact.

Battle Mages, who hold the position of “Artillery Officer”, fight at the longest range possible. They wear similar equipment to archers, but unlike every other soldier of the Yellow Institut, they have wide brim white sorcerer hats and have a myriad of books connected via chains to their belt. These books grant them power to cast more advanced and more dangerous magicks. The primary spell of choice is “Gelbaer Ellosien”, a spell with which the Artillery Officer holds their palms close together. Between their palms, floating, a small black ball of fire will appear with a blinding sickly yellow core. It is then condensed and condensed until it is smaller than a fist, where it is then launched at ungodly speeds in a slow arcing motion. Shortly after taking flight, it begins to split into various other projectiles, all of which create large explosions of rapidly spreading fire upon impact.

An Artillery Officer is only given such a title due to the importance of their position, but they hold no true authority. As for engines of war, the Yellow Institut employs the use of culverins, ballistae that fires engraved incendiary bolts, springalds, and trebuchets. At their hips rests a curved espada made of a non-iron metal called Blaugrava. These weapons, more ornate and elongated, are somewhat saber-like, though the quillon is abnormally long and curved downwards and then up again, designed to catch another sword with ease. These swords are called Qaer, and Qaeren when referring to multiple of these swords.

Verwalación

The Verwalación act as a police force of sorts, though they care more for manhunts, searches, and patrols. They answer directly to the Kapitel von Jueces, acting as law and order. Their main goal is to root out any potential trespassers of their lands that may have somehow slipped past the Gray Walls.

Adorned in sunbleached black coats, they wear a burnt yellow shoulder cape over the left shoulder. A sheathed, ornate Qaer rests at their left hips, ready for a quick and easy draw. The sheathes, a white and red cloth folded over polished oak containers, have an iron-absorbing metal on either side of the resting blade, meant to absorb the blood off the blades. Their gloves and boots are of a black, carefully made smooth leather, especially hardy and meant to denote authority. Over the right breast they wear a white and red emblem of the Kapitel von Jueces.

Upon finding an individual that does not belong, their espadas are quickly dispatched, returning to their sheathes bloodied and ready to be cleaned.

They are made up of guilds, one in each city. The guilds are typically built as great blue and gray buildings, almost like a small fortress, with a dungeon system built below each one to contain non-trespassing criminals. Their hierarchy is basic, but effective. Every region of the nation is divided into districts, where each guild possess control over specific sets of districts. Each district is assigned a Sargento, who each commands a veritable force of men dependent on the number of districts and the population of those districts.

Above each Sargento is an Eisen Juece, who has all of the Sargentos of each guild fortress answer to them, meaning there is only one Eisen Juece per city. As a council, they make collective decisions for the betterment of the Verwalación, though they still answer to the Kapitel von Jueces.

Kapitel

There are a myriad of Kapitel, each presiding over various things. A Kapitel for law and order, one for finances, one for agriculture, one for guilds, one for building, one for non-food resource gathering, one for citizens, and so on. Each theoretically has the same goal, but they often end up butting against each other.

Kapitel von Jueces

The Kapitel von Jueces, those who control the Verwalación, dictate order and enforcement of that order. They often work with the Kapitel von Gente, using their archives to determine lawful citizens and trespassers. They care only for the enforcement of law, though this often means that they step on the toes of other Kapitel.

Kapitel von Gente

The Kapitel von Gente are interested, for the most part, in the organization and management of the people. At each birth, records are created for a person and stored in white and green marble towers, made so to prevent them from being set alight with fire. A detachment of special Verwalación, these wearing the white and green emblem of the Kapitel von Gente instead of the white and red of Kapitel von Jueces, stand guard around each archive and at certain locations throughout the archives. Their yellow capelets have been replaced with vibrant blue with white marble lines, sewn in with silk. The cloth about their Qaeren have been replaced by black cloths adorned with brilliant teal patterns.

Each person, if desiring to travel from one district to another, must make their way to their local Reisebüro - again, guarded by the Gente Verwalación - and pay a small tax, state their intentions for records-keeping, and gain a permit to pass through and to any and all districts involved. A permit is carefully and magically inlaid upon the citizen’s anterior forearms, showing as a vibrant blue to any who choose to inspect it. The permits fade from sight when not being inspected, though they fade after the approved amount of time, making them null and void.

Kapitel von Negocio

The Kapitel von Negocio are often at odds with Kapitel von Gente, since Kapitel von Gente makes their jobs notably more difficult and time consuming. Their primary interest is in tax collecting and trade, which requires frequent travel across several or dozens of districts. In each major city, they have a Gigante Halle, a tall pantheon-styled building of blacks, whites, and silvers.

Guarding their Gigante Flure, the plural of Gigante Halle, are the Silber Custodiar. They have a simple black uniform with a large white leather cloak about them, fastened with multiple yellow sashes, kept closed. There are slits on either side of this cloak to allow free movement, though a curved pauldron-band of silver sits on the shoulders, connected to each other with an ornate yellow rope. At their hips are extremely ornate Qaer, though sitting in polished redwood sheathes with a white cloth band around them and silver engravings along the sheathes. Atop their heads are a simple black cap, tilted forwards and to the right, with the emblem of the Kapitel von Negocio on the front of it.

The Silber Custodiar are silent for the most part, as they are trained to be quiet, and they only allow authorized individuals and officers through, even if it means crossing blades with the Verwalación due to not letting the Verwalación into the buildings.

Tax collectors and other important officials are often escorted by one Silber Custodiar, as it wouldn’t do to let a tax collector be murdered in the streets by a disgruntled citizen.

Kapitel von Crecimiento

The Kapitel von Crecimiento are typically at odds with both the Kapitel von Gente and Kapitel von Negocio. They wish for free movement between agricultural districts, made difficult by Kapitel von Gente, and they provide tax-breaks to farmers, putting them at odds with Kapitel von Negocio. However, due to their granting of tax breaks for growing wheat, raising cattle, or other such things, they are one of the few Kapitel that citizens actually like working with, even if they still have to obtain permits for what they can grow, where they can grow, and when they can grow their products - all of which could take years to obtain due to the queues and processes needed.

Kapitel von Mar

The Kapitel von Mar use Monstrulción, giant ships, a mix between cargo and warships. Below the first deck, the first and second lower decks, highly specialized culverins, called komverinsch, line the sides of the ships and are manned by Krieg Custodiar. The function of Kapitel von Mar is to maintain naval dominance of the surrounding seas, transport goods from the mainland of the Yellow Institut to a few colony islands, and to hunt leviathans.

The ships they use, all called Monstrulción, are specialized depending on their function, though none possess unique or identifiable traits. They are manned by sailors, armed by Krieg Custodiar, and led ship-wise by a captain and combat-force wise by a Silberkrieg Custodiar. A komverinsch, the machine of war that the Krieg Custodiar use, fires a long, billowing black and yellow flame, meant to engulf other ships. Their range is just shy of a normal culverin, though those are kept aboard as well.

For the Monstrulción, those adapted to hunt Leviathans, the Krieg Custodiar mount “Rot” Harpoons, machines that fire a specialized harpoons that delve deep into the seas. Each harpoon is taller than three men, shaped to slice through the water without difficulty.

The Kapitel von Mar cares little for the issues or squabbles of the other Kapitel, though they strongly dislike the Kapitel von Negocio due to the increased tax on Leviathan hunting. There is no care for the Leviathans themselves, but successfully killing one may provide a fortune.

Shrikes

The Shrikes, otherwise known as Those Who Came First, are a race of elf-people and the oldest race known to this world, immortal save for murder, untouched by disease, sickness, and blight. They are a pale and inhumanly tall people, quiet and bitter. Once, they existed all over the world, no corner was untouched by them, but as the other races began to appear, they were eventually driven back to the Obsidian Plains in the North-West of the continent of Krashq’kanov, otherwise known to the common people as Kanov.

The Obsidian Plains are barren lands, scars left upon the world from a duel eons ago between a few Gods, long since left scarred for no God would care about fixing what they broke. The lands, large and flat, are broken up only by cavernous mountain ranges, torn open with gaping holes to swallow entire cities. It’s cold, but there is no snow. They are wet mud-lands, covered in gigantic flat plates of obsidian that stretch for as far as the human eye can see - including the mountains.

Left with these barren lands as their only home, the Shrikes swore a hatred against every other living race. The world those younger races walk upon does not belong to them, it belongs To Those Who Came First.

Empress

Es’esfuras, or more simply known as Esfur, is the Empress of the Shrikes. She, like her people, has long, thin pointed ears pointed off to the sides of her head and drooping downwards. Her hair is a jet black, reaching clear down to her knees, and her skin is an odd corpse-white pale. Her eyes are a cold black, marred by cold disdain. Like many female Shrikes, Es’esfuras stands at just over 187 centimeters.

She wears the traditional garb of Shrike nobility, grays and blacks and whites, all slim and allowing free movement with strategic slits. About her shoulders rests a black capelet, trim adorned with small gray feathers - believed to provide the wearer with the ability to see through lies. Her staff, a blackwood polished pole, adorned with obsidian and a Vanhr Shrike’s skull at the very top.

Near eight thousand years past, Es’esfuras slew the previous Empress, Bar’yanak, and assumed the throne, blessed by Niy’yaga himself. In doing so, her clan became Gosyr’tsar, as all clans who ascend the throne do, in order to preserve the royal line. Those previously who were known as Gosyr’tsar become outcasts at best, targets of persecution, and their skulls become desirable for anointed decoration. Such a recent event has sent the various clans into a frenzy, though they have calmed to the public eye lest they draw Es’esfuras’ disdain and summary execution. She is young, they believe, but her act of slaying Bar’yanak proved her title as the most powerful sorceress among the Shrikes a true title.

Now, she waits, slowly shifting the Shrikes deeper into the North of the Obsidian Plains, where the armories and forges grow larger and the great Kev’t’untergrad - great spyre-like towers - swell with new students. Soon, she whispers to herself, the Cleansing will begin.

The Ranges

To curb the disturbance of outside forces, Empress Es’esfuras ordered various clans to form “ranging” parties, which came to be known as Ranges. Each group is made of no more than fifty Shrikes, all armed with vanhr piercers, to scout the plains and the borders of the plains. They are unseen, for their stealth is unmatched, and their keen eyesight allows them to see from a much further distance without being seen in return. Their vanhr piercers are fashioned after crossbows, or perhaps crossbows are fashioned after vanhr piercers. Vanhr piercers are much, much longer than a crossbow, enchanted with runes of silence to prevent their position from becoming known, runes of lightness to reduce the draw weights of their piercing quarrels, and runes of seeking to hit the target of the shooter’s ire.

Upon finding anything that does not belong, be they great beasts, members of the Kranov Enclave, wanderers, adventurers, or anything else of the sort, these Ranges will take up positions from their great distances with their vanhr piercers and eternally silence the intruders. Upon finding attempting invading armies or crusades of the Anointed Holy Cities, they will do the same. However, instead of closing the distance to inspect their kills, they will fall back to the great Cavern Cities and report their findings to stir up more Ranges to aid them.

Niy'yaga

Niy’yaga is a bitter Greater God of death and the father of the Shrikes. They were his most favored creations, from which he stole death from so that they may never suffer. He is often absent, long and gone, only to appear as he wishes, coming and going, though he anointed the crowning of Es’esfuras.

Embittered at the creations of other gods and goddesses, of pantheons new and old, punishing and hurting his children. In observing this horrific acts, he granted his children one final gift, the gift of sorceries. Of enchantments. Of runic magicks. Then, further, aware of their vulnerabilities, he made this gift much stronger in his daughters.

His form, a tall, slim skeleton, of pale teal, draped in a flowery blue and pink rose robe, wielding a simple Shrike-like bonespear. His head, instead of a standard Shrike or human skull, is that of a leopard skull, the same as those leopard familiars he gifted his children in the early Age of Wonderment.

Yhs'nohs'roljke

Yhs'nohs'roljke is the name of a great academy, made of a multitude of Kev’t’untergrad, lead and run by the clan Ihs’roljke. Ihs’roljke, forbidden and unable to own any lands, is still one of, if not the most powerful, clans among the Shrikes.

They lead the most prestigious academy of sorcery, of which every clan head, clan heir, and figure of note has studied in Yhs’nohs’roljke. Despite it being an academy of sorcery, it has multiple divisions, one of which for sorcery, one for alchemy, one for mage weapon crafting, and one for the arts of war. Studies can take as little as three hundred years to as much as just under two millennia, such is the complexity of the subjects.

Highly accomplished and esteemed alumni are gathered, outside of clan duties, to create a council called the “Miskrada”, a council that directly advises the Empress, whoever she may be, without bias, seeking only to serve her goals, whatever they may be. However, the continued neutrality is skin-surface at best, and many unfavorable alumni may find themselves missing.

Important Figures

Matron Friy’krasmin

Friy’krasmin, Friye for short, is a shorter Shrike woman, standing barely at 178cm, being the newest and youngest Matron of Clan Ultr’Ranov, ruling for a score-and-three thousand years. After slaying her mother and ascending the seat of Matron, as all Matrons do, Friy’krasmin helped Es’esfuras get into position to become the Empress due to their mutual friendship and interests, as Friy’krasmin is one of the new Empress’ most staunch supporters.

Her clan relocated from their cavernous home to the south-west of the Obsidian Plains to the central north of the plains, near enough to the Empress to provide aid as she seeks and far enough to shield her and her clan from the accusations of other clans. To obtain their new home, Friy’krasmin wished not to wait, and so ordered a war and prompt execution of Clan Yhs’Jakova, carried out over the course of two weeks. However, as no nobles remained to testify against Clan Ultr’Ranov, no crime had been committed and no wrong had been done.

They fly the banner of the Vanhr Shrike, quartered black and teal with a pink skull of the Vanhr Shrike.

Anointed Holy Cities

The Anointed Holy Cities began long ago, in times untold, as the beginnings of a myriad of pilgrimages from a myriad of peoples. Over time, they began to create cities that grew so large that they became mountains and, eventually, larger than mountains. Their capital and greater cities were built were they believed their holy lands to be, only for their worship and devotion of their believed to be Father of All to grow corrupted. Slowly, they began to rot from the inside-out, until they were left a carcass of zealots and the worst kinds of people. To live is to devote oneself, to die is to repent.

Inner Sanctum

The Inner Sanctum is located in the hidden midst of the Great Rim Cities, far beneath the surface of buildings, and accessible only by hidden pathways and guarded gates. As one walks down the dark pathways, the air grows thicker, older, and harder to breathe as the scent of rotting paper and ink grows stronger.

Finally, once light is found, it is by lantern-light, in a giant labyrinthine maze of a library. The shelves reach upwards farther than the eye can see, though severely inhibited by the extreme amount of dust in the air. Large, rounded, hunched over hooded fingers, taller than the common man, slowly roam between the walls of books and scrolls. Whatever color their cloaks might have once been, they have aged into being a dust-coated and worn brown. From their robes, an arm holding an iron lantern is cast, carefully controlled so that fire may never escape it, while a series of other arms - one, perhaps two being organic, the rest being wooden - roam through the countless pages and texts.

These are the Keepers of the Inner Sanctum, librarians of the most odd sort, records keepers, and these are they who give the Inquisition and Holy Sisters information of all sorts.

Inquisition

The Inquisition, at the highest level, are figures of near supreme authority, second only to the crown. They hunt through the Anointed Holy Cities, routing out heretics and blasphemers alike, before deciding what fate awaits those who turn away from the One Faith. They are not forbidden from fathering children, only forbidden from straying from their duties.

Every Inquisitor is taken as a young boy, be they from beggars, orphanages, homes with too many sons, or noble houses as tributes. These boys are then taught every art of perception, of information gathering, the arts of inflicting pains, of swordplay (or other various weapons), every facet of the One Faith, and so on, until they become men. Once they become men, they become a Low Inquisitor, paired with a Holy Sister of similar rank, and dispatched to the outer cities, towns, and villages. Here, they act as typical Inquisitors, as secret police, and as beast hunters, though only towards the particularly daemonic threats.

After they have proven themselves, they may become True Inquisitors and, while they maintain all their previous duties, they may accompany or shadow along High Inquisitors. Only the truly exceptional may become High Inquisitors through the apprenticeship of other High Inquisitors. Once they have achieved this rank - though most do not - they are second only in authority to the crown.

The High Inquisitors roam the thirteen Anointed Holy Cities, stalking their streets and alleyways to eradicate those who might become enemies of the One Faith. Throughout the entirety of their tenure as an Inquisitor, regardless of rank, they are always paired with a Holy Sister of similar rank.

Holy Sisters

The Holy Sisters are seen as the pinnacle of the One Faith. As babes, they are taken from a myriad of sources - just like the Inquisition - before being raised carefully and gently. They are taught every facet of the One Faith, every verse in their sacred texts, every hymn, every prayer, every spell granted to them by the Father Above All, how to dispel the darkness that are daemons, and so on. They are not forbidden from birthing children, only forbidden from straying from their duties.

As young women, they become Living Sisters. Here, they are paired with a Low Inquisitor, and are dispatched to outer cities, towns, and villages. However, they are not constantly with the Low Inquisitor, and often reside in the local chapels, abbeys, and parishes. Here, they silently and discretely report on their findings, including their partner Inquisitor, to ensure that he may never stray too far.

As they serve, they will become Lesser Priestesses, True Priestesses, All-Seeing Sisters, and, if they are truly talented and exceptional, they may become a Low Archangel. A mark of being a Low Archangel, however, is that they must also be a Saintess, a title acquired by great deeds - either saving a large group of people, helping to annihilate a greater daemon, or some other such thing. They will typically follow along with their partner as they go, however, they often cycle through multiple Inquisitors as they do so because too many Inquisitors fall away from the One Faith enough to be permanently removed.

A Low Archangel is the highest position of the One Faith, and there may only be thirteen at a time - one for each city - and they are seemingly immortal. Once they die, however, they will ascend into becoming a true Archangel in service of the Father Above All.

Noble Houses

The Anointed Holy Cities are ruled by hundreds, perhaps thousands, of noble Houses and Great Houses. Each House and Great House that wishes to continue existing must swear itself as a Banner-House to the crown, sworn to call upon hosts and knights and all manner of soldiers for war whenever the crown calls. Each Banner-House’s summoned hosts are the militaries of the great Anointed Holy Cities.

Great House Arkithane

House Arkithane is one of the many Great Houses of the North-Eastern Anointed Holy City of Ephernaum, one of the merchant cities. House Arkithane largely controls much of the lakeside and riverside and water-way trade routes, leaving them relatively rich. They are, however, intelligent enough to make a more than necessary tithe to the One Faith, granting them both immunity and protection by the Inquisition and Holy Sisters.

Their surcoat is a quartered surcoat of white and a dark turquoise with a white and black emblem of the Hand Of The Eighth Saint in the middle, a Saint said to be the elder brother of the founder of House Arkithane that aided in purging the North-Eastern lands for the occupation of the Anointed Holy Cities. House Arkithane is the third Great House to pledge itself as a Banner-House to the crown.

They are a solemn and hostile Great House to the other Great Houses and commoners, fortifying their Great Houses, Manors, and Estates - which they have four of - and guarding them heavily. Even their maids are trained in the arts of death.

Their maids, referred to as “Silents” due to House Arkithane removing their tongues and refusing to call them by whatever name they might have once had, are dressed in a thin but extremely modest uniform. Their faces are covered in a smooth black face-plate mask. Each has a small-headed axe with a basic handguard, as they are expected to eviscerate any intruders on sight.

House Arkithane holds aggressive, financially expansionist habits, yet they always ensure that they have a way out of a bad deal or a way to spin things in their favor. Due to their cunning, ambition, and loyalty, they are one of the chosen favorites among the Bannermen to the crown. When the crown calls, they can call forth hosts up to seven-thousand strong, focused on small axes, polehammers, and archers.

By axe and polehammer and arrow, by the might of the One Faith, every coin that touches the water shall belong to House Arkithane.

Great House Kernobohlg

House Kernobohlg is one of the many Great Houses of the Anointed Holy Cities and the second Great House to declare itself a Banner-House to the crown. Many of the sons of Great House Kernobohlg serve or devote themselves to the Inquisition, though never the eldest son of each generation so that the Great House may continue onwards.

They hold the western coasts of Mothua, serving as devoted wardens, yet they are among the first of the houses to join Holy Crusades or even help incite Holy Crusades. Unlike other Great Houses that may pay a generous tithe to the One Faith, Great House Kernobohlg pays a tithe of half its income, leading to extreme favor from the various priests of the One Faith, the Inquisition, and the Holy Sisters. Kernobohlg cares little for the internal politics of the Anointed Holy Cities, for these are true zealots.

Great House Kernobohlg may summon hosts of forty-thousand strong. Five thousand Knights, two thousand siege machines, a score and nine thousand common men, and four thousand others for various purposes. In warfare, deserters are not merely imprisoned, they are publicly executed and made an example of.

There will be no retreat, no cowardice, no defeat. No white banner, no mercy line. No last warning, only advance by design.

House Tarbairn

House Tarbairn is an odd noble house, though “odd” in that it is viewed upon with disdain even by the likes of the other Banner-Houses of Leviathas. They reside just outside Leviathas, as many of the Banner-Houses of lesser note typically do, claiming the lands round about the Cliff Keep and Cliff Keep itself. Their banner, a white field with a green bridge, upon which a great gold lion holding a spear stands upon it, flies all round-about their lands.

Tarbairn specializes, surprisingly, or unsurprisingly, in pyrotechnics and in the use of tar - such was their namesake. They prefer to lure armies and enemies into ravines and canyons and manufactured divides, only to reign fire and pour tar and oil over the poor foes beneath. They would sooner gift the entirety of their wealth to the One Faith and Inquisition than face their foes in open, direct combat. They are a contemptible sort, often perceived to lie or backstab, though most notably they view the word “mayhaps” as a loophole of sorts.

They can summon hosts, though not of the same size as Great Houses. The largest host they’d ever summoned seven hundred, two score and ten armed peasants and scarcely more than ten knights.

Leviathan Mines

The Leviathan Mines are a giant, cavernous mine construct deep within and underneath the Anointed Holy City of Leviathas. Here, the Abyssal Cults - they who call themselves the Jegari - fester, though they are kept on a leash and periodically cleansed in an attempt to get rid of them. Though, just like rats, they always somehow reappear.

The miners are kept under strict control, led by task masters who wield scourgestaffs. A scourgestaff is a simple, cruel weapon, meant more to inflict pain than to actually kill. The threads of the scourges are long and thin, the heads of which are also long and thin, beaded in slim diamond-shaped sharp beads of scorched stone, meant to make them more durable and intimidating. The beads have several sharp points, meant to rip into skin, but not so much that they dig into flesh or cause major damages. The staff portion itself is to give the weapon greater range and an additional weapon in case the mining slaves attempt to rebel.

An excerpt from a letter from the superintendent of the Albin Quarter of the mines to the quartermaster of the shipment cranes,

“... A shipment of five fothers of Blackfog Glass, a score-and-eight fothers of stone, and ten-and-nine fothers of marble will be made to the Southern Ascended Peak for preparation. As of this quarter, only a handful of corrections have had to be made, only a smaller shipment of new fodder will be required. The activity of the cults has remained culled, for now, though an investigation from the Inquisition may be required yet again soon, though no monstrous beast has been spotted as of late…”

Inquisitors are often dispatched to investigate and then “cleanse” the mines with approved holy sources to stamp down insurrections, cultist uprisings, and any creatures that the cults may summon.

Figures of Note

High Inquisitor Belial Mizrahi

Belial Mizrahi is a rare Inquisitor in that he does not have a partner Holy Sister, though he is regarded as a living Saint for helping to slay a greater abyssal daemon in Leviathas, his home. Here, he presides over the Leviathan Mines. His face has long become shadowed and hard to see, but his red cloak is a dead give-away. It is said that in times of great need, due to a blessing granted to him by Low Archangel Rachael, golden wings of light appear to protect him from the most lethal of blows. He is silent, yet his presence is physically painful enough to send others screaming away from him.

High Inquisitor Ezekiel Khalas

Ezekiel Khalas is a brutal, obsessive man. Long ago, he would have strayed away from the One Faith, only to be paired with a Holy Sister by the name of Seras, a Low Priestess at the time. It is true that he no longer inwardly cares for the One Faith, but Seras does, and she is the pinnacle of beauty and love to him, reinforced when she chooses to lie with him for his particularly great deeds. From then on, he was utterly obsessed with performing her every whim, and has long since received a myriad of blessings from her for his acts of service and all deeds heroic in the eyes of the One Faith. He is a tall, gaunt man, with all skin and muscle burned away from his jaws, leaving a horrid skeletal smile there, too wide to be natural. His eyes are a glowing pale maroon without pupils.

He wears the traditional High Inquisitor garb, though altered with a sky-blue capelet in secret honor to Seras. At his hip lies a sheathed executioner’s blade, a strange obsidian similar to the myriad of obsidian tablets imbedded into his left forearm. At his other hip hang a myriad of chained books, each a holy script, to give him power to shield himself as needed. To any and all else other than Seras, he is a cruel, horrid man, delighting in the execution and persecution of any he deems lesser. One must run from him should they wish to keep their head, for Ezekiel cares not if they are heretic or otherwise, as he will use the slightest provocation as justification to display the wrath of the Father Above All.

Low Archangel Seras

A truly gentle woman, a rarity, and the only Low Archangel of recent memory to have only ever had one Inquisitor partner, one which she loves most - second only to the One Faith. She is a short, petite woman, with long hair flowing down to the ground, white as snow - though not from age, it is said that her hair has always been such. She wears the traditional garb of a Low Archangel, the traditional black and blue robes, and with her she carries a black staff with the emblem of the First Saint upon the head of it. She is a forgiving woman, kind and soft spoken to the most desperate for healing, only for her to gently whisper and turn unrepentant blasphemers to dust in an instant.

She has had many children, each “belonging” to Ezekiel Khalas, though true to her dearest desires, she has sent each away to become Inquisitors or Holy Sisters themselves. Her inner court, as all Low Archangels possess an inner court of Holy Sisters, located in the Southern High-Peaks of the Great Rim Cities, consist solely of her children from across the ages.

People Of The Far-Dunes

A land and peoples of endless heat, painful spice, poison, and creatures of unnatural design, The People Of The Far-Dunes are several nomadic caravans that travel the vast Far-Dunes, known to have various sorcerers, warlocks, alchemists, seers, oracles, and all other strange and unknown practitioners of the mystical arts. Not much is known of them, but what is known is that the Far-Dunes beget magicks and malice. All that is known is a rough bestiary made from those who survived encounters with various caravans and roaming nomads.

Djinn

There have been a few Djinn seen - and barely survived against - from those unfortunate enough to witness a summoning by a Greater Seer. These are they.

He Of A Thousand Hands

A Greater Djinn, known as He Of A Thousand Hands, is a mysterious figure. He stands nearly thrice as tall as a man, though permanently seated cross-legged is he, held aloft by a myriad of outstretched hands. Around He Of A Thousand Hands is a single pair, yet endless at the same time, number of arms and hands out-stretched all around, as though odd wings.

His skin, an ink black, with a face smooth as stone and calm water, is like that of an endless void. His white and yellow robes flow down to the sands beneath him, yet do not touch, leaving behind no trail. He Of A Thousand Hands has been seen few times, but enough to be known, that neither armor nor shield will prevent his cruel tricks.

A great knight, once foolish enough to stand his ground, had giant ink-sharp arms sprout from his own chest. From the torn flesh and blood, the hands descended upon his other limbs, quickly ripping and twisting and tearing them from the torso. His men fled as quickly as they could, but those who were too close suffered the same fate.

Golden Wheel Bearer

The Golden Wheel Bearer is only slightly larger than a normal man, yet he carries upon his back a magnificent, cruel, gleaming spiked wheel. Gigantic in nature, it’s made of an ashen black wood gilded gold with emerald etchings. His visible skin is a sickly purple, though his face is covered by a great smooth golden half-mask with sun-spikes adorning it. Below his ribs, all manner of flesh and blood vanishes, leaving only an odd and inhuman skeleton spine and oddly spiked pelvis. Partially on the pelvis is a pair of black sash emerald-green pants, leaving bony purpley flesh shins, ankles, and feet visible at the end of said clothing. The Golden Wheel Bearer carries the great wheel upon his back with four great arms despite it dwarfing him in size alone, leaving long lines in the scorching sand.

The Golden Wheel Bearer is seen as an executioner, summoned by the Great Seers and Warlocks to crush and bind his unfortunate targets by and onto the Golden Wheel. He is eternally silent, said to exist purely as The Hand Of Death.

Plated Visages

The Plated Visages were first seen summoned by a Great Seer who had been ambushed. Strange, giant plates of various faces - if some could even be called faces - appeared about the Great Seer. Some of stone, some of metal, some of wood, and some of materials unknown, all in different designs, shapes, and sizes.

A great stone face with closed eyes, billowing cheeks, two sharp horns from the right side of the forehead and one from the left, floated above the earth. It was the size of five men and spewed forth a strange and scorching yellow-red flame. Another visage, of wood and glass, depicting a sorrowed ram, spewed forth a bolt of lightning. Another among them summoned a great wall of earth, and so on.

Abyssal Cults

The Abyssal Cults stem from a nomadic tribe of the People Of The Far-Dunes, comprised of warlocks and summoners, who were defeated and captured by a raiding party from the Anointed Holy Cities. Now, enslaved for hard physical labor in the mines of the abysses below the southern cities, these people turned to worship of the abysses and the horrors that lurked within.

Far below the surface, in the leviathan mines, where water has turned to a murky, clogged ink and blood mixture called corpse-water, pouring down from the surfaces and streams, they began. Their homes, measly stone-carved shacks dug into the tunnels and cavern walls, surround the Black Lake like a ring. Beneath the surface of the Black Lake, there are untold skeletons and corpses of the fallen, discarded and thrown away to be removed from the walkways and work-spaces of the mines.

Within these dead depths, the cultists, calling themselves the Jegari, named after he who discovered what lied within. At the end of each offshooting river, each stream, each simple trickle of corpse-water, lay a puddle or a pool. The Jegari circled themselves round about these puddles and pools, using half-finished rituals of summoning long-forgotten, to draw forth creatures and horrors to return all to their rightful place: dead and drowning in the sanctified corpse-water.

One day, one day soon, they will drive back the accounting maesters, the task masters, and the soldiers from the Depths Far Below, and then they will be free to summon from the Black Lake what Jegar once saw: The Tooth-Faced Moon. For in summoning it, it shall devour endlessly and ceaselessly, for life is nought and should return to nothingness, free of pain.

The Flaying Courts

The Flaying Courts are fox daemons ruled by a Greater Goddess known only as the Blood Empress, who lives for, and loves only, herself and inflicting pain. Each court of the Flaying Courts worship different aspects of pain. Cruelty is their sustenance, pain is their delight, and skin is what they reap.

Court of Agony

The Court of Agony, built around pain of the physical body, is within the First Open Sky. The ground, covered in green grass, is seemingly made of jagged glass in the shape and coloration of glass. The tall grass is shaped so to cut and knick at various arteries and muscles, designed to make you add yet another red trail to the countless other red trails within the First Open Sky.

It is best to be quick, however, for no traveller is alone. Wandering this sky are black-as-pitch humanoid figures, once people, long since disfigured and transformed into something entirely different. Their skin has vanished, replaced with a thick black flexible glass with large, long, jagged blades of glass jutting out from various places. Their only goal is to find a traveller, embrace them, and drag them towards the floating dens. The sludge they inject through their glass blades will keep any unfortunate enough to be embraced alive.

Inside the floating dens roam crude, fox-like daemons, clothed in fine drapes of skin. There, the travellers shall have their skin slowly stripped, their muscles liquified, and forced to remain living whilst the flexible black glass is poured onto them, all in a prayer of degenerate pleasure to the Empress of Blood.

Court of Loss

The Court of Loss, within the Second Open Sky underneath the First, may only be reached upon finding the Well of Descension, a well ringed by unknown and countless limbs, grafted into the insides.

Upon descending the well, a new sky can be found high above. The sky, dripping with a sick yellow and covered with a blanket of black cloud, is broken only by the giant pillars of limbs of creatures known and unknown, reaching from the heavens to seemingly endless voids in the earth itself. The rivers and lakes cannot be trusted, for it might be water, but it might be corpse-water. The earth may be solid one moment, but the instant a traveller’s guard is down, the earth shall open to consume them.

Here roams the Daemons of the Mind. They crawl and skitter along, oddly small and snake-like, yet still fox-like, until they wrap around the traveler and attempt to dig into their skull, implanting visions of suffering, of loved ones dying, and of utter despair, purely for their own amusement.

Court of Bells

The Court of Bells is located within the Third Open Sky, the entrance of which is the Well of Jingling. The Well of Jingling is located within the middle of a shallow puddle-lake of corpse-water. Instead of a proper well, the Second Well is a giant hole in the earth, ringed with endless chains, with the air below obscured by a gray mist.

The land beneath is plain and barren, an obscene flatness of ink black sand, cool to the touch with the moisture in the air. Hanging from the heavens here are chains larger than most towers in width, vanishing high above into the mist, darkened by giant rings that become visible, ringing bells the size of small towns, loud enough to shatter the hearing of anything nearby.

In long procession lines, vibrant blue four legged fox daemons, each standing on their hind four legs, walk. They are adorned in the skins of the fallen and slain, carrying tall black staffs lined with ringing bells atop the top end. They will capture any and all they come across, be they creature, man, or fellow daemon, and work at destroying their senses of hearing.

The Blood Empress appreciates the prayers of pleasure that the Court of Bells devotes to her, but finds disdain in their lack of creativity and seeming disconnect to the pleasure of it all.

Court of Lightless

The entrance to the home of the Court of Lightless, the Fourth Open Sky, is a white oak pillar streaming with blood. Ringed around this pillar is an emptiness, a void, where one must climb down the pillar to see what awaits them. This is the Well of Vision.

The skies are a twirling mass and mess of reds and blues and greens and yellows, forming an endless number of eyes, forever watching with baleful beams of hued light and pain cast from each unblinking eye. The ground twists and heaves violently, yet it is perfectly flat. It bends, but remains narrow and straight. It moves, yet it remains perfectly still. It causes pain just to watch it.

Daemons of this realm seek to capture those unfortunate enough to make it that far, dragging the screaming fools deeper and deeper into the realm. Pillars, made of exotic stones that descend into corpses and bodies either absorbed or carved into the pillars and adorned with great white spikes, are the destination of these captures. Once there, the fox daemons will impale their victims on these spikes, facing outwards and up into the violent hued gazes of the eyes high above. The spikes, made only to contain the victim, keeps them perpetually alive, though it does not prevent their victims from rotting.

Far below, ringed about these pillars, dance the daemons - for their joy is great, and all shall see the source of their pleasure.

Court of Instance

The Fifth Open Sky, accessible by the Well of Ascension, is accessed by a valley-sized pit hidden deep within the Fourth Open Sky.

Far below, color ceases to exist. Warmth is a pleasantry of the past, coolness is a long-forgotten desire, and mere comfort has died long ago. Between odd slabs of white stone are embedded white corpses. Some deformed, others not, some with missing fingers, some with their skulls sliced in two, others impaled, and so on. There is no roof, reaching only endlessly and out of sight into an unfathomable black void, high above. Those who somehow make it to the Fifth Open Sky will eventually realize that the ground is not made of stone, but of flesh and bone and sinew, the same white of their surroundings.

All is dim, yet one will eventually adapt. But they are not alone.

The Kurogitsune, the fox daemons who call the Fifth Open Sky home, are the favored children of the Empress of Blood. Each footfall upon the fleshy ground is a calling to them. Slowly, they stalk and hulk along, tall and gaunt. Standing on their hind legs, their black fur is a stark contrast to their surroundings, though it’s still difficult to perceive them due to the general dimness of the Fifth Open Sky. Their many arms each wield various skeletal, jagged blades, each personalized for the Kurogitsune’s personal enjoyment and preference.

Upon making contact with the foul intruders, the Kurogitsune behead, impale, or eviscerate their prey with glee. The fallen, now abandoned even by the Kurogitsune in search of more prey, will slowly rot and fuse into the ground itself.

Blood Empress

Long ago, in the Age of Divinity, there was a meagre goddess, a deity of small note, the local deity of spite. No matter what she did, no matter how she accomplished tasks of note, no matter how many other pantheons she helped topple, she was overshadowed by another goddess, otherwise known as the Lady of Pride.

At first, they had been twins, and the best of friends, but being overshadowed had begun to show the cost. Jealousy writhed through her being, and the little goddess began to plot and to scheme. First, she allied herself with the God of Pain. Then, the gods and goddesses of resentment, joy, catharsis, and of twisting came to be her allies - some willingly, some forced, others negotiated with. In secret she worked, gaining power as steadily as she could, becoming more degenerate and spiteful, more hateful and cruel.

Then, once again in secret, she began to consume her allies. First pain. Then joy, then resentment, then twisting, and finally catharsis. Each secret murder gave her power beyond measure, and yet she did not show her hand to the Lady of Pride. Not yet.

After the Lady of Pride and her inner circle conquered another realm, this meagre goddess invited the Lady of Pride and her inner circle to dine and feast in a great banquet, hosted in the honor of the Lady of Pride. Gleefully, the goddess accepted, thinking none of it - after all, this was her rival, her sister, her best of friends, her closest confidant.

The meagre goddess poisoned the inner circle, cursing them to die with their food and their drink, and poisoned the Lady of Pride with a powerful concoction that removed the will of her limbs and her voice. With great glee, the meagre goddess exposed her plan, and began to work her art of pains and joys upon the Lady of Pride, taking a grim catharsis in doing so. When the deed was done, the Lady of Pride’s soul was imbued within her own skull, which was then detached and re-fashioned into a chalice.

Thus began the First Age of Madness, marking the birth of the Empress of Blood, the kinslayer. From her will, she created the Kurogitsune, freakish fox-like monstrosities and daemons that served as her children, her most precious designs. She traveled the lands round about, turning all who crossed her into trinkets and trophies, keeping them alive within, eternally showing her superiority over they.

She settled in the Darklands, deeming it to be the most pleasant and beautiful place, before twisting it and establishing her Great Courts, eventually coming to be known as The Flaying Courts. None approach, lest they become the subject of the Empress of Blood’s degenerate and filthy pleasure and become living pieces of pained art.

Flajhills

A race of thin giants, one of the youngest races of this world. They are rather peaceful and unfortunately kind and naive, often leading to them being exploited, captured, and enslaved for physical labor. Their homes are usually found high above in the mountains of Fahroway and Mothua.

The primary forces that enslave the Flajhills in Mothua, the Anointed Holy Cities, will often leave the Flajhills true homes alone to allow the Flajhills to continue reproducing and, as such, create more able bodied workers. The reason they are often left alone is so their naivete remains and so they are more pliable. Every few years, small slaver parties of the Anointed Holy Cities will hike into the mountains, choose a small village, and herd off that small edge village as quickly as they can, lest they be noticed.

In Fahroway, most Flajhills actually live fairly peaceful lives, so long as they stay in their mountain homes. They learned early on that those that wander to go see the strange sights down below the mountains vanish in a strange, baleful hue of yellow and black flame.

The Fahroway Flajhills, unable to leave, turn inwards. They have simple chiefs for each village, though they primarily farm and work the wood round about them - large redwoods and gor oaks, named after Gor Funderlen, the first among them to learn the arts of woodworking. They build strange houses, each using tall slab-like pillars of redwood and gor oak, with roofs made of what some would call an upturned water-vessel. Between many of these villages lie large bridges, held together of wood, stones, and leathery sinew of the few livestock the Flajhills possess.

Monolith Clockworks

There are a great many giant, odd, floating towers, strange in architecture and bizarre, not matching any one style, floating in the South-Western portions of the continent of Krashq’kanov. These towers, long, long ago, were created by a host of Wizards and Warlocks and all manner of masters of the arcane arts, said to be a library-city of towers, in an attempt of progress.

They created their peoples, a people not born of flesh and blood, but constructs given life through magic. Puppets of all shapes and sizes and styles, made of bone and of wood and of metal and all manner of things, given consciousness. These puppets, called Clockworks, served as the workers, guards, and soldiers of the floating Monolith towers.

However, the greed and lust for knowledge of the founders grew too great, and the curse of the world struck them down. Accidents became common, falls and slips that no rational or irrational being would make snuffing out life, jealousies and petty rivalries turning into wanton murder, and so forth, until no flesh and blood being was left, leaving only the Clockworks themselves. The Clockworks, after a time, learned how to create more of themselves, and so overran the now barren Monolith towers.

All unfortunate stragglers, adventurers, tribes, caravans, and all manner of people that happen upon the Monolith towers, are welcomed with open arms. However, as the Clockworks wished to learn how to create puppets of flesh and of blood, such as what they believed humanity consists of, they disassemble all those they come across - willingly or otherwise - attempting to figure out how they work. Such attempts are in vain, however, for the Clockworks were never given all the knowledge of their creators, and, as such, are incapable of understanding.

Soubtar

Soubtar is a puppet of fire, made to be a protector of the Monolith Clockworks, a skeletal brown construct wearing white and red holy robes. In his right hand he wields a staff, long and odd, with a strange lantern atop it. It primarily acts as a staff and torch, though the top of which ends in a melted open barrel, as though it spews flame.

Soubtar, during the Second Age of Madness, a few eons after the Alchemists long past had died, descended from the Monoliths. After all, they were safe enough for he and his ironkin. He traveled, long and far, observing and watching. In his travels, he deemed that the living puppets were inferior and that there was no point attempting to understand them, as they took glee in destroying each other.

Upon returning to the Monolith of Gerukiy, he shared what he saw and found with the other constructs. Those who listened, stayed, and altered themselves in the Ways of Fire as he was. Those who refused to listen, or simply did not believe, left the Monolith of Gerukiy and traveled to the other Monoliths. Now, surrounded by his followers, who he named after himself, they created an armed force, ready to defend the lands of the Monoliths. However, their beliefs led them to be cast out from the lands of the other Monoliths, restricting them to just the lands and monolith of Gerukiy.

After a wayward army of odd flesh puppets, touting off about some holy crusade, Soubtar himself fell in combat, leaving him a puppet martyr of Gerukiy, strengthening and heightening their resolve and frenzy.

Chitinous Enclaves

The Chitinous Enclaves are made up of various tribes of hunting bugmen scattered all throughout the world. Not much is true amongst all of the tribes other than each tribe takes great import in hunting and becoming the apex predators of their environment. Some tribes are more bug-like and some are more humanoid, but all are bug-like. A Greater Hive-Mind, the Great Mother, rules over each tribe, but not each individual. The control is enough to give each tribe a direction, though none outside of the Chitinous Enclaves know where the Great Mother originates from.

Kranov Enclave

The Chitinous Enclave doesn’t have much of a presence in Krashq’kanov, but the presence it does have is far more inhuman than most of their brethren. Their ideal prey are the great beasts of the region, Shrikes, the combat constructs who wander away from the great Monoliths, and various villages and towns.

Their exoskeletons are typically a glossy, obsidian black, with mud-green colorations between each outer plate of the exoskeleton. Unlike most of their brethren of the other Enclaves, the Kranov Enclave uses giant, thin, spined pincer claws and spined skeletal tails, all of which having an even tougher exoskeleton plating. As with all Enclaves, the Kranov Enclave knows that might makes right, and he who slays the greatest foes and beasts shall command the Enclave.

Mothua Enclave

The Mothua Enclave is unique among the Chitinous Enclaves in that it was among the first spawned by the Great Mother. They were among the first to walk the lands and were the first people to name themselves on those lands - Mothua - before they named the lands after themselves.

Thin, elegant, moth-like beings, pale as moonlight. They seem almost fragile with such slim and spindly limbs, such fragile looking wings, frail antennae, porcelain white exoskeleton plating, and human-like face plates. Their clothes weren’t hardy either, made of the soft and silky-smooth antir mycoid skins gathered and skinned from antir mushrooms. Their weapons were neither large nor bulky, either, being made of sharpened and carefully shaped and sharpened antlers, wrapped in antir mycoid skin-leather and adorned with special blue-bells or other such plants, made to never die and to remain in full bloom.

The Mothua Enclave, living in the eastern forests of Mothua, reside in a forest that has come to be known as the Ancient Wood. The forests here have grown strange and warped, tree canopies grown so thick that sunlight may never touch the forest floor. Here, the Mothua Enclave can outrun all, leaping from tree to tree, using strange moth-like wings to aid them in leaps, bounds, and glides. They use the vibrant antir mushrooms almost like a spider-web to track their prey or to sense intruders.

Their weapons drip with various poisons, though it is mostly for show. What is seen dripping is little more than flower-dyed tree sap, for the real poison seeped and soaked into the antlers themselves long ago.

Unlike most of the Chitinous Enclaves, the Mothua Enclave does not need to hunt the largest or most terrifying beasts, for they know what holds true power: the strange humans in their lands, able to adapt to any situation, to flood any other force or creature with such numbers that the likes of which cannot be comprehended. No, the Mothua Enclave will slink along by night in search of officers, Inquisitors, Holy Sisters, members of noble houses, and so on, aiming to assassinate those for the simple joy of watching it cripple the odd, screaming, wriggling creatures that call themselves humans.

Ekternau Enclave

The Ekternau Enclave, residing in and around the large islands of Ekternau. They are primarily crustacean-like, not so much insectoid, unlike most of their brethren. On top of their exoskeletons, they wear the plate-like bone fragments of whales and great fish like additional armor, segmented and tethered together with whale sinew, believed to provide strength and durability.

They use take their old exoskeletons, break them down, and reshape them using sinew from large fish and carved driftwood to create gargantuan, odd weapons. One such weapon seen was a re-used sharpened claw mounted on a large staff, while another seen was a large slab of armor mounted on a shorter pole of driftwood, sharpened and serrated.

Their intelligence is above that of those like the Kranov Enclave, but below those of the Mothua Enclave. They primarily hunt great whales, squids, leviathans, and the various peoples of the Ekternau islands. Occasionally, the Ekternau Enclave will travel to the western shores of Mothua to hunt the strange, ink covered humans there. None of the humans are particularly powerful there, but their numbers are numerous enough to give the Ekternau Enclave a good time.
