Tale of Creation

Long before the Age of Divinity was a being known only as the Father Above All, though some eventually came to call this being the Father of All or other such variations. He came to see a vastness of nothingness, displeased yet bored was he at this vast nothingness. To begin remedying this, he created his children, the First Gods, the Greater Gods.

First, he created his sons, the Greater Gods of Creation, Neutrality, and Death. Then he created his daughters, the Greater Goddesses of Destruction, Bias, and Life. Each of they he allowed free reign, though he used their aid in creating a small piece of earth. From this piece of earth, all else grew, expanding outwards in size, consuming all of the vastness where once was nothingness. Great seas and oceans, vaster than a mortal eye can fathom, began where once was dry earth. From raised portions, life began, and finally from these the heavens were created. Here, he divided the heavens from the earth, giving his children homes and free will to do as they wished.

Content with what was, he gave his children a final gift, the gift of names.

  • To his Son of Creation, he named he “Ljolnir”.
  • To his Son of Neutrality, he named he “Waehatl”.
  • To his Son of Death, he named he “Niy’yaga”.
  • To his Daughter of Destruction, he named she “Musanshi”.
  • To his Daughter of Bias, he named she “Iljhayf”.
  • To his Daughter of Life, he named she “Almictus”.

With this act, he gave free reign of this new world to his children, though the name he gave it has long since been lost to time. No longer needed, he turned inwards, and gave to this world the ultimate sustainer of life, the first sun. This sun, a palace of grandeur and wonderment, of amazement and horror, of beauty and strife, of paradise and hell, he called home, never to return to this world again.

Their Father Above All gone, the First Gods settled in different portions of the world. The Son of Death, Niy’yaga, was the first to create his own children, a tall, long-lived people, to which he gave the vastness of the empty worlds. With them, he gave all his love, granting them blessings upon blessings, doting upon them as his own father did not. Thus began the Age of Divinity.

Not to be outdone, Ljolnir, Musanshi, Iljhayf, and Almictus created their own children in their own pockets of the world, though each sired a court of lesser Gods. Ljolnir, the Son of Creation, made the first and all Titan-folk. Iljhayf, Daughter of Bias, created all forms of humanity, and so on. Over time, these courts further divided, becoming pantheons, each doing as they saw fit. Their creations began to crawl and scream forth from across the world, taking territories from Niy’yaga’s favored children, his most blessed and cherished of all. This, he vowed, would not go unpunished.

Seeing the growing contentions and soon all-out war, Waehatl resigned himself and looked down upon the others and their barbaric ways, creating for himself a palace that would come to be known as the Screaming Moon, where he would reside for the rest of eternity, undisturbed and unbothered by his siblings petty bickering.

Great wars wrought themselves upon the face of the mighty earth, scarring the lands, destroying, twisting, remaking what once was into something new and chaotic. Niy’yaga was driven back, cast out and into hiding, while Iljhayf was betrayed by her own courts and pantheons, marking the first act of Kinslaying.

As time went on, the fights and squabbles between the First Gods and their pantheons eventually calmed and soothed into a mere seething, quiet, cold disdain for one another, ending their wars as they receded and left to their own corners of the world. Thus was the world left disfigured, no longer aligned with the Father Above All’s intent, but he would not intervene, for he gave his children the freedom of free will and agency, and now they would live within the nightmare they created.

Thus ended the Age of Divinity.

Nadim ibn Iljhayf

Nadim ibn Iljhayf, otherwise just known as Nadim, is one of the many sons of Iljhayf. He is a tall, slender, lean being. Having no eyes and no nose, the majority of his face is somehow completely smooth. His mouth, full of pointed teeth, sharp and razor-like, spreads in an ear-to-ear grin, though more often than not it is controlled. He possesses two horns, coming from the sides of his head and going backwards, both curved, the right horn being drastically longer than the left. Additionally, he has three arms, each long, slender, and disproportionately sized for his torso, having a single arm for the left and two arms, side-by-side and not below nor above one another for the right, and his skin is a vibrant red. Below his waist he keeps a cool, dark grey toga, undone and left purely at the waist and legs, accented in a forest green.

As strange as he looked, Nadim was made the Lesser God of Rationality. His words, however, were smoothed out and soft, gentle, almost, becoming persuasive with ease, as he was raised side-by-side with Qolaya, the Lesser Goddess of Apathy and one of Waehatl’s few children. She, a being of eternal beauty, captured Nadim’s heart and mind from the very beginning, though he was more akin to a gentleman when it came to dealing with her.

One of the other Lesser Goddesses, She of Spite came to Qolaya, whispering and scheming, hidden so that Nadim may not know. She whispered about what Iljhayf would do, what she planned, about an unseen plot to kill Waehatl. With this fresh in her mind, Qolaya approached Nadim, telling him of this plot, and how Iljhayf was too dangerous to be allowed to live.

Believing her, in all his blindness, Nadim went to his mother, making calm accusations, entrapping her. She denied, just as Qolaya said she would, and in turn, Nadim plunged his dagger through Iljhayf with frightening speed, watching as she fell. The other Lesser Gods and Goddesses present, most of which part of the scheme but not publicly so, captured Nadim for his crime of kinslaying.

In the final moments of freedom, he cried out to Qolaya, only for her to watch and then, finally, walk away, as he was lowered into the Realm of Agony, and later made into a living mummified corpse decoration for the Lesser Goddess of Spite to be kept in her living palace. Alive, he can scream without noise for eternity, silently begging Qolaya to pull him from his new hell.
